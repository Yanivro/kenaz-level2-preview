What we explored

Where we started: the parts reflection

One person shares freely. The other listens for parts and reflects back: "This is what I hear. It might not be right. You've got this part, this part, and this part. How is it to hear that?" It isn't about being right or wrong. It starts to untangle what was one big knot.

You may notice your own parts getting triggered as you listen, through resonance or resistance. Notice it, and don't let it take over.

From a ball of string to a sweater

The purpose of IFS is what we call Self-leadership: a deeper, Self-led relationship with our parts. Parts want to be seen, understood and acknowledged. They don't want to stay in the shadow. They want to be an integrated part of the system.

Think of a ball of tangled string, and a sweater where you know every stitch. We can't turn the ball into a sweater until we've untangled it. That's the first step. When there's a relationship with a part, we can go further, toward the place where it's frozen in the past. A critic isn't talking to you as you are today. It's still protecting a child.

Kindergarten teacher or parent

The kindergarten teacher

"At some point this one will go away. The parent will come and pick them up." It's easier: someone will rescue me, or I'll be able to push this part aside.

The parent

"I am the one responsible for this part: to nourish it, to work with it, to love it." It's harder, and it's what Self-leadership asks of us.

When a child screams in the supermarket aisle, part of the parent hates the child in that moment. In healthy parents, that part isn't driving the bus. Part of them is challenged, and part of them still loves and cares. We learn to turn toward both.

There are no bad parts.

All parts, even the ones we hate, are at their core trying to protect us and help the system. They're using strategies that were crucial in the past and aren't serving us now. Because those strategies hurt, other parts hate them, and that's why we end up saying, "this part is holding me back."

Knowing isn't enough

I might know in my mind that a monster part is really a little child, because I read it in all the books. That doesn't mean I feel it, or can relate to the part that way. We have to embody it, and that means practice.

Why a facilitator helps

It's much easier for me to bring love and care to your inner critic than it is for you. So I help by modeling it, and by being someone willing to see the part while you watch. I'm bringing my Self energy in. If your part triggers me, I can't do that, which is why this work starts with our own parts.

Guide

The process: befriending a part

This is the first step of doing IFS with someone. We practiced the first three Fs in the session. Befriend and Find out take it further. You can do this with a partner, one of you in each role.

The explorer

Turn inward and say out loud what you notice. Everything is optional, including answering.

The companion

Read the questions slowly, then wait. No fixing, no advice, no interpreting. Silence is good.

After each F, ask: Have we finished this F? How do we know?

  1. 1Find

    Notice which part is here. Noticing it already helps it unblend. We often begin with the old view: "It's annoying me, it stops me from being who I am." Direct the person to find the part, through a sensation in the body or an image. We call this a trailhead. Just one small thing is enough. There's no right or wrong.

    What we say

    "What part would you like to get to know today? Maybe the one that's hardest to be with."

    "How do you experience this part? Is there a sensation in your body, or an image?"

    How we know to move on

    You can say "there's a part of me that..." and point to how you sense it.

  2. 2Focus

    Focus expands Find. What matters here is that the person doesn't go into the mind and interpret, because that moves away from the present moment. Come back to the embodied experience, and use imagination.

    You can offer words, like tingly, stabby or dense. Even when they're wrong, they help: "It's not dense, it's actually more airy." The more tangible it gets, the more there is to relate to.

    What we say

    "Find the experience in your body. Is it OK to focus on it now?"

    "What sensation? What color? Is there a sound, a shape, a weight? Does it move? Is it heavy or light? Is there a kinetic energy?"

    How we know to move on

    Your attention is resting on it, and you've noticed at least one more detail about it.

  3. 3Feel

    This is the crucial step, because it tells us where to go. Now there's me and there's the part, and I can notice our current relationship.

    What we say

    "How do you feel toward this part?"

    "Is there space for it?"

    How we know to move on

    You can name at least one of the 8 Cs toward it, even faintly.

    If not, see "When there's no space yet".

  4. 4Befriend

    Create the relationship, and let the part know you're here.

    What we say

    "Let the part know you'd like to get to know it."

    "Is it aware of you?" If not: "Invite it to notice you're here."

    "Take your time. Be with it in whatever way feels right."

    How we know to move on

    The part seems aware of you, and you feel some contact with it. It doesn't have to trust you yet.

  5. 5Find out

    Ask the part about its job, and let the answer come from the part.

    What we say

    "Ask the part: what's your job in the system? What are you hoping to do for me?"

    How we know we're done

    An answer came from the part, not from your thinking. If it isn't ready to say, that counts too.

If something young or very tender shows up

Don't go there today. Let it know you see it, ask it to turn the volume down, and promise to come back to it with support. You can stop at any point.

Closing the round

  • Explorer: thank the part, and any part that stepped back for you.
  • Companion: "Being with you this whole time, I feel..."
  • Explorer: "What it was like for me was..." Then switch roles.

The 8 Cs of Self

These are signs of Self energy. One is enough, even faintly.

  • Curiosity
  • Calm
  • Clarity
  • Compassion
  • Confidence
  • Courage
  • Creativity
  • Connectedness

Sometimes you can steer what's here toward a C. "I feel sad for it." "Do you feel compassion toward it?" If yes, there's some Self energy, and we can keep relating to the part.

When there's no space yet

If what comes up is "I hate it", "I want it gone", "It's right about me" or "I should be more compassionate", then another part is standing in front of Self energy. Nothing's gone wrong.

  1. Notice the part that's reacting

    "Can you sense the part that hates or fears it?"

  2. Understand it, and let it know

    "Does it make sense why it feels that way? Let it know you understand."

  3. Ask it to soften

    "Are you willing to soften or relax, just for a few minutes?" The yes has to come from the part, not from what I think. So take a moment and feel into it.

  4. Check again

    "How do you feel toward the first part now?"

  5. If it isn't willing, it becomes the new focus

    "This is the part that needs space right now. Is it OK to turn toward it instead?" We don't push parts away, so go back to Focus with this part. There's no way around it. It may need to be understood, and to release some of what it has carried, before it trusts Self with the first one.

How to choose a part

  • Feel into your body and notice what's alive right now.
  • Choose a part you're challenged by, like anxiety or an inner critic.
  • Ask someone: "Which part do you feel most curious about, or most resistant to?"

If someone moves between many parts, you can name it: "I notice you're moving between parts. Is there one you'd want to focus on?"

You first have to learn how to draw before you can paint.

This is a creative process, and at the start your brain has to work and comes in front of your flow. You have to learn to use the brush before you can let it use you. So stumble in the dark. It might not go perfectly, and that's great.

In the rooms

What I wrote during the practice, and why

While people practiced, I came into the rooms and sent companions private messages. You'll see them on screen in the recording. Here they are together.

"Ask if they understand how the part feels."

Create space for the part to feel whatever it's feeling, without needing to change. That creates more safety for parts, once they know Self has space and understands the feeling without trying to change it.

"Ask what the experience of the fear is. What is the sensation?"

We want to move away from thoughts, interpretations and explanations, and into embodied sensation in the present moment. So we focus on the experience of the emotion and the sensation.

"Ask if they can share the compassion with the fear."

Once you notice there's Self energy, you want it to be not just in the Self but part of the relationship. When we share that compassion, the part is touched by the energy of Self, not just observed or witnessed by it.

"And if it's able to receive it."

Notice the experience of being touched by the energy of Self. If it's true Self energy and the part isn't blocking it in some way, it will have some kind of positive effect.

"Let it know it's OK that it feels this way."

We can feel whatever is here, whether it's fear, pain or anything else, and it's welcome. We'll be with it, and we don't want to change it.

"Can you ask if the anger can relax for right now, and soften?"

So the feeling of protection can dissipate in this moment, since there was nothing to protect from. It helps us connect better with the part when the protector isn't in the way.

"See if they can share the compassion with the child."

The same as above: the part is touched by the energy of Self.

What these have in common

From thoughts to sensation. From noticing Self energy to sharing it. And from trying to change a part to making room for it as it is.

Your questions

What came up afterward

What's the objective? I can sit and have tea with a part. What happens then?

Self-leadership. First we untangle and relate, as you're doing. Then we can go to the place the part is most afraid of, where it's frozen in the past.

If there's something negative toward a part, is that still Self energy?

It means there's another part, holding some negative energy. Self energy emerges naturally in all of us. The question is how to access it.

Is anger always negative? Mine felt protective, almost like Self.

I don't think anger is negative. A part that wants to protect is beautiful. And I ask myself: is this relevant to the present moment? Is there something to protect from right now? Usually there isn't, so I ask if the anger can relax while we explore. Anger is a protective emotion, so there's something underneath it, maybe sadness or fear.

What if two parts want opposite things?

That's a polarity, and we can ping-pong between them for a long time. When we meet each part and understand what it wants and why, we often find their purpose is very similar: to help and protect us. In a technique we call the boardroom, you sit both parts down with Self. One speaks while the other listens, and then they switch. You're a kind of couples therapist for your parts.

Did I choose the wrong thing to focus on?

There's no wrong way to focus on a part and try to understand it. Think of Snape in Harry Potter: we hate him until we learn his history and intentions. It's the same with a part. It's not just you listening. All of your parts are the audience. The more the system knows a part, the more it can connect with it, and with Self.

We are a self-correcting system

Correction happens by itself, but the system needs to observe that there's something to correct. When we see a problem and don't want to look at it, we stop our own corrective powers from coming in. Even starting to separate, "this part and this part are here," can bring more Self energy and more curiosity.
