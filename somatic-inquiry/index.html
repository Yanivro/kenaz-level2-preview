Somatic Inquiry for Breathwork Facilitators

When a client says “it's anxiety”, the label can end the conversation. Learn a simple way to guide them back to what they actually feel, and practise it live.

Is this for you?

For the moment after the breath, when something is STILL there.

  • A client finishes a session in a challenging place, and you're not sure how to hold them.
  • They name it (“fear”, “anxiety”, “my trauma”) and the story takes over from the body.
  • You worry that focusing on a hard sensation will only make it stronger.
  • You want a few clear questions you can trust, without fixing, pushing or analysing.
The tool

Set the label aside. Stay with the experience.

It sounds backwards: turning toward a hard sensation tends to settle it rather than make it bigger. When a feeling is met with attention and breath, instead of a story about it, it often softens, moves and makes room. We'll show you why, and how to guide someone there.

The interpretation
I feel anxious. It's the same fear that always comes.
→
The experience
There's a tight band across my chest. It's warm, and it pulses when I breathe in.
The hour

Short on theory, long on practice.

You'll practise in pairs, guiding and being guided, so you leave with something you've already used.

01 · Why

Naming isn't feeling

What happens when we interpret a sensation, and why attention to the raw experience regulates instead of escalating.

02 · How

The felt sense

The qualities a sensation can have, such as location, shape, temperature, texture and movement, and the questions that bring them into focus.

03 · Practise

In pairs

Breakout rooms with simple prompts. You guide, you're guided, and you notice what shifts.

04 · Deeper

When it's a part

A short look at how a sensation can be a part that wants to be seen, and where parts work takes this further.

A taste

A few of the questions we'll work with.

Simple enough to use the same evening, with any client, after any session.

  • Where do you notice it in your body right now?
  • If you set the word “anxiety” aside for a moment, what's actually there?
  • Does it have a shape, a temperature, an edge?
  • What happens when you breathe toward it?
